Why we sleep better with calm sounds

AION Browser demo article · 5 min read

Many people fall asleep faster when they listen to quiet music, rain or a calm voice. Steady sounds mask sudden noises from the street or the house, so the brain is less likely to wake up.

Scientists call this sound masking. A constant, gentle background makes a door slam or a passing car feel less sharp, because the difference between silence and the noise becomes smaller.

Slow music around 60 beats per minute can also lower the heart rate and help the body relax. Many listeners choose classical pieces, ambient music or nature sounds for this reason.

Light matters too. Bright screens late at night can delay the release of melatonin, the hormone that tells the body it is time to sleep. Using dark mode and lowering brightness helps.

Finally, it is better when the sound stops by itself. A sleep timer pauses playback after 15, 30 or 60 minutes, so the music does not play all night and the battery lasts until morning.

In short: calm sound, low light and a timer form a simple routine that helps many people fall asleep and sleep through the night.
